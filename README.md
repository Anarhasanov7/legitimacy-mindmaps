## The thesis in one paragraph

Judges (investors, clients, institutions) hold a mental yardstick for what a "real business" looks like. Founders send signals; judges deliver a verdict. Scholars have studied each piece separately — but nobody has tested whether **training founders** to send better signals actually changes the judge's verdict. This thesis runs that test: a field experiment where half of the founders in the ATENA program get a 12-hour legitimacy-training module and half don't.

**The chain:** Expectations (judge's yardstick) → Conformity (founder's signals — *what the module trains*) → Assent (judge's verdict — *what Siraz measured*) → legitimacy conferred.

## The 5 legitimacy states (Siraz et al. 2023)

Legitimacy is not yes/no — it's a spectrum. **Where ATENA founders start: Unknown/Undecided.**

1. **Legitimate** — nobody even questions it (a bakery on your street)
2. **Conditional** — accepted *if* you meet conditions (GMOs with labels)
3. **Unknown** — judge can't decide, three sub-types:
   - *Undetermined* — invisible, nobody's looking → fix: get noticed
   - *Undecided* — looked at but unplaceable → fix: give a familiar frame ("it's like X for Y") ← **the module's job**
   - *Polarized* — half love it, half hate it (crypto) → fix: different framing per audience
4. **Conditionally illegitimate** — improper in principle, tolerated for necessity (medical cannabis)
5. **Illegitimate** — actively rejected (scams)

Key insight: a venture's state can flip purely because the *societal yardstick* shifted — no change in its own behavior.

## The method in brief

**Design:** randomized field experiment (Article 1) + interviews explaining the why (Article 2) + repeated surveys (T0 → T1 → T4).

**The 10 steps, condensed:**
1. Define variables on Schoon's dyad (train conformity, measure assent)
2. Adopt Bertrand's self-legitimacy scale + a perceived-legitimacy scale
3. Power analysis *before* recruiting (~100–175 per arm for d≈0.3–0.4)
4. Recruit → T0 baseline for everyone → randomize → separate cohorts
5. Deliver the 12h module: **5 capabilities** — Cognitive (fit a known category) · Pragmatic (show value) · Social (get vouched for) · Factual (show milestones) · Regulative (show compliance)
6. Survey both arms at T0/T1/T4 + collect objective milestones + pitch decks
7. Track dropouts (leaver/stayer comparison + imputation + ITT)
8. Analyze: ITT → TOT → DiD → effect size + confidence interval
9. Interview high *and* low responders; Gioia coding; second coder + kappa
10. Write it as the missing link: Siraz proved judges respond to signals; Campos proved training works; the connection was never tested in the field.

**Every problem has a fix:** small sample → pre-registered power analysis · attrition → leaver/stayer + imputation · contamination → cohort separation + staggered rollout · self-report → objective milestones + text scores · coding subjectivity → dual coders + kappa · CMV → temporal separation + varied formats.

## The numbers that matter

- **Campos et al. 2017 (Science):** psychology-based training → **+30% profits**, traditional training → +11% (n.s.). A short behavioral module beats content training — exactly what the 12h module is.
- **McKenzie 2021 (meta):** business training averages **+10.1% profits** across all RCTs. Key line: *"not finding a significant effect is not the same as finding no effect."*
- **Pollack et al. 2012:** founder preparedness (trainable!) → legitimacy → funding amount — the thesis chain on real money.
- **Tornikoski & Newbert 2007:** legitimation *actions* predicted venture emergence more than founder traits — what you DO beats what you ARE.
- **Siraz's evidence base:** ~20,000 conjoint ratings across 4 experiments.
- **Rule of thumb:** n per group ≈ 16/d² for 80% power.

## The committee's hardest questions — short answers

- **"Just a gap nobody filled?"** No — a theoretical tension: legitimacy lives in the *judge's* head, training operates in the *founder's*. Theory can't predict whether signals cross that boundary.
- **"What if you get a null result?"** Still a contribution: it proves founder-side training can't cross the boundary → policy should target judges/platforms instead.
- **"Isn't it all self-report?"** No — objective milestones + Ghods text-scoring of pitch decks + a planned conjoint study with real bank officers.
- **"Why Bertrand's scale, not self-efficacy?"** Self-legitimacy measures *entitlement to be a founder*, not skill — it's what triggers impostor syndrome.
- **"Won't bias override training?"** Possibly — that's why subgroup analysis (gender, origin) is built in. A ceiling effect is itself a finding.
- **"France ≠ Italy?"** Ghods shows legitimacy representations differ by context → modeled as a formal moderator, not pooled.
- **"Why only 12 hours?"** Clean causal identification — long incubators confound training with peer effects and mentoring.
