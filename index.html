Can Training Founders Change How Judges See Them?

A plain-English digest of the legitimacy mind map — the argument, the evidence, the experiment, and the defense.

Open the interactive mind map → GitHub repo

The thesis in one paragraph

Judges — investors, clients, institutions — hold a mental yardstick for what a "real business" looks like. Founders send signals; judges deliver a verdict. Scholars have studied each piece separately, but nobody has tested whether training founders to send better signals actually changes the judge's verdict. This thesis runs that test: a field experiment where half of the founders in the ATENA program receive a 12-hour legitimacy-training module and half don't.

Expectations→Conformity→Assent→Legitimacy The judge's yardstick → the founder's signals (what the module trains) → the judge's verdict (what Siraz measured) → legitimacy conferred.

The 5 legitimacy states (Siraz et al. 2023)

Legitimacy is not yes/no — it's a spectrum.

1 · LegitimateNobody even questions it — a bakery on your street.
2 · ConditionalAccepted if you meet conditions — GMOs with labels.
3 · Unknown — where ATENA founders startThe judge can't decide. Undetermined = invisible (fix: get noticed) · Undecided = seen but unplaceable (fix: give a familiar frame — "it's like X for Y" ← the module's job) · Polarized = half love it, half hate it (fix: different framing per audience).
4 · Conditionally illegitimateImproper in principle, tolerated out of necessity — medical cannabis.
5 · IllegitimateActively rejected — scams.

Key insight: a venture's state can flip purely because the societal yardstick shifted — no change in its own behavior.

The method in brief

Design: randomized field experiment (Article 1) + interviews explaining the why (Article 2) + repeated surveys (T0 → T1 → T4).

  1. Define variables on Schoon's dyad — train conformity, measure assent.
  2. Adopt Bertrand's self-legitimacy scale + a perceived-legitimacy scale.
  3. Power analysis before recruiting (~100–175 per arm for d≈0.3–0.4).
  4. Recruit → T0 baseline for everyone → randomize → separate cohorts.
  5. Deliver the 12h module — 5 capabilities: Cognitive (fit a known category) · Pragmatic (show value) · Social (get vouched for) · Factual (show milestones) · Regulative (show compliance).
  6. Survey both arms at T0/T1/T4 + collect objective milestones + pitch decks.
  7. Track dropouts (leaver/stayer comparison + imputation + ITT).
  8. Analyze: ITT → TOT → DiD → effect size + confidence interval.
  9. Interview high and low responders; Gioia coding; second coder + kappa.
  10. Write it as the missing link — Siraz proved judges respond to signals; Campos proved training works; the connection was never tested in the field.

Every problem has a fix: small sample → pre-registered power analysis · attrition → leaver/stayer + imputation · contamination → cohort separation + staggered rollout · self-report → objective milestones + text scores · coding subjectivity → dual coders + kappa · CMV → temporal separation + varied formats.

The numbers that matter

+30%profits from psychology-based training — Campos et al. 2017, Science (traditional training: +11%, n.s.)
+10.1%average profit effect across all business-training RCTs — McKenzie 2021 meta-analysis
~20,000conjoint ratings across Siraz's 4 experiments
n ≈ 16/d²per group for 80% power — the rule of thumb

Pollack et al. 2012 showed founder preparedness (trainable!) → legitimacy → funding amount — the thesis chain on real money. Tornikoski & Newbert 2007 showed legitimation actions predicted venture emergence more than founder traits — what you DO beats what you ARE. McKenzie's key line: "not finding a significant effect is not the same as finding no effect."

The committee's hardest questions

"Isn't it just a gap nobody filled?"

No — a theoretical tension: legitimacy lives in the judge's head, training operates in the founder's. Theory can't predict whether signals cross that boundary.

"What if you get a null result?"

Still a contribution: it proves founder-side training can't cross the boundary → policy should target judges/platforms instead.

"Isn't it all self-report?"

No — objective milestones + Ghods text-scoring of pitch decks + a planned conjoint study with real bank officers.

"Why Bertrand's scale, not self-efficacy?"

Self-legitimacy measures entitlement to be a founder, not skill — it's what triggers impostor syndrome.

"Won't bias override training?"

Possibly — that's why subgroup analysis (gender, origin) is built in. A ceiling effect is itself a finding.

"France ≠ Italy?"

Ghods shows legitimacy representations differ by context → modeled as a formal moderator, not pooled.

"Why only 12 hours?"

Clean causal identification — long incubators confound training with peer effects and mentoring.
